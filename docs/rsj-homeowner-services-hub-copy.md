## H2: Signs you need a plumber

Most expensive plumbing repairs start as something small that went unaddressed for a season. If you're seeing any of these in your home, it's worth a call before it becomes a bigger job.

### H3: A drain that keeps clogging
One clog is normal. The same drain clogging every few weeks usually means buildup deeper in the line, a partial obstruction, or a problem in the main sewer line. Plunging and store-bought drain cleaners treat the symptom and can damage older pipes.

### H3: Water where it shouldn't be
Stains on a ceiling, a soft spot in drywall, a musty smell in a closet, or a water bill that jumped without explanation. Hidden leaks do the most damage because they go unnoticed the longest.

### H3: Low or uneven water pressure
A single fixture with weak flow is usually the fixture. Low pressure through the whole house points to a supply-side issue — a failing pressure-reducing valve, a leak, or mineral buildup in older lines.

### H3: Sewer smell or gurgling drains
A sewer odor in the house, or a toilet that gurgles when the washer drains, means air and wastewater are going somewhere they shouldn't. This is a main-line warning sign and should be looked at quickly.

### H3: Water heater acting up
Lukewarm water, hot water that runs out fast, rumbling or popping from the tank, rust-colored water, or moisture around the base. Most tank water heaters last [10–12] years; many homes built in St. Charles County in the [late 1990s and 2000s] are on their second or third unit, and a lot of those are due.

### H3: A sump pump you haven't thought about
If you haven't heard your sump pump run in a while, or you don't know how old it is, it's worth testing before the next heavy rain. Sump pumps in this area work hard, and a failed pump shows up as a flooded basement.
