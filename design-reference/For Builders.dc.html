What we do on your job

Most of our week is builder work. We're set up for it — the trucks are stocked for rough-in, the crew knows the inspectors, and Ryan bids and walks every job himself.

Bid and plan review

Fixture takeoffs from your plans, value-engineering suggestions where they save you money without hurting the finish, and a number you can put in your budget. [Turnaround: # business days from receiving plans.]

Underground and rough-in

Underground before slab, then supply and DWV rough-in coordinated with your framing schedule. We show up when the framer's done, not a week later.

Top-out and inspections

Top-out, pressure tests, and rough inspections in St. Charles County and its municipalities. We schedule the inspection and we're on site when the inspector is.

Trim and punch

Fixture set, final connections, walkthrough with you and the homeowner, and punch-list items closed before you ask.

How we work with builders

Same crew, start to finish. The plumber who does your rough-in is the plumber who does your trim. No rotating subs, no re-explaining the job.

One point of contact. Ryan's cell. Schedule changes, plan revisions, a question from your super — one call.

Daily check-in when we're on site. Your super hears from us at the start of each day we're on the job: what we're doing, what we need, when we'll be out.

We handle inspections. We schedule them, we meet them, we fix anything the inspector flags without you chasing us.

Paperwork ready. Certificate of insurance, W-9, and lien waivers [on request / with every invoice — confirm]. Master Plumber license on file.

Clean job site. Our cutoffs and packaging leave in our truck.

Project types

  • Custom homes — full plumbing from underground through trim, including tankless and recirculation systems, multiple baths, and fixture packages the homeowner picked.
  • Spec and production builds — repeatable rough-ins on schedule, priced per plan.
  • Whole-house renovations — re-plumbing occupied or gutted homes, relocating fixtures, tying into existing systems that don't match the drawings.
  • Additions and basement finishes — new baths, wet bars, and laundry rooms tied into existing drains and supply.
  • Kitchen and bath remodels — working alongside your tile and cabinet trades on their schedule.
